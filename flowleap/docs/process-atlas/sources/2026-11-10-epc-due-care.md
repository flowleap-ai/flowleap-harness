## 1. Art. 122 and Rule 136 EPC

1. Art. 122(1): an applicant or proprietor who could not observe a time limit "in spite of all due care required by the circumstances having been taken" has rights re-established on request, where the missed time limit directly causes a loss of rights. SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/en/legal/epc/2020/a122.html
2. Art. 122(3): if granted, the legal consequences of the missed time limit are deemed not to have ensued. Art. 122(4): no re-establishment for the period for requesting re-establishment. Art. 122(5): third-party rights for good-faith use in the interval. SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/en/legal/epc/2020/a122.html
3. Rule 136(1): the request must be filed in writing within two months of the removal of the cause of non-compliance, and at the latest within one year of expiry of the unobserved time limit. Special case: for Art. 87(1) priority and Art. 112a(4), two months after expiry of that period. The request is deemed filed only when the fee is paid. SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/en/legal/epc/2020/r136.html
4. Rule 136(2): the request must state the grounds and the facts on which it relies; the omitted act must be completed within the request period. SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/en/legal/epc/2020/r136.html
5. Rule 136(3): re-establishment is excluded for any period for which further processing under Art. 121 is available, and for the re-establishment period itself. SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/en/legal/epc/2020/r136.html

## 2. Guidelines for Examination 2026, E-VIII 3.2 (merits of the request)

6. "All due care" means all reasonable care: the standard of "the notional reasonably competent proprietor, applicant or representative". Cites T 30/90. SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2026/e_viii_3_2.html
7. Due care is met if the non-compliance "results either from exceptional circumstances or from an isolated mistake" within a normally satisfactory monitoring system. SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2026/e_viii_3_2.html
8. Where an isolated mistake is alleged, the party must show "the monitoring system normally works well". Such a system "must include an independent, effective cross-check mechanism". The Guidelines state this requirement does not apply to relatively small entities or patent departments (cites T 166/87, J 11/03). SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2026/e_viii_3_2.html
9. The duty applies first to the applicant and passes to the representative by delegation. The applicant is not exempt from "the consequences of their own mistakes". The representative is presumed to supervise his own work continuously (cites T 1095/06). SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2026/e_viii_3_2.html
10. Routine tasks (typing, posting letters, noting time limits) may be delegated to assistants. The representative must show that "assistants have been carefully selected, duly instructed and periodically supervised". The same strict standard is not applied to the assistant (cites J 5/80). SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2026/e_viii_3_2.html
11. Decisions cited in E-VIII 3.2 (2026): T 30/90, T 166/87, J 11/03, J 3/93, T 112/89, J 19/04, T 381/93, T 1095/06, J 5/80, T 439/06, J 3/88, J 4/07. SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2026/e_viii_3_2.html
12. E-VIII 3.2 does not mention computers, software, electronic or automated systems. SOURCED (absence, as reported by the summarising fetch tool over the full section text). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2026/e_viii_3_2.html

## 3. Case Law of the Boards of Appeal, 11th ed. (2026 revision), chapter III.E.5

Structure: III.E.5.1 submissions; 5.2 general comments on due care; 5.3 exceptional circumstances; 5.4 isolated mistake within a satisfactory system for monitoring time limits or for processing mail (5.4.1 to 5.4.7, of which 5.4.4 "Requirement for a cross-check" with a) large firms, b) small firms, and 5.4.7 "Deficiencies in computerised systems"); 5.5 persons required to exercise due care (5.5.4 "Due care in dealing with assistants", a) to e)). SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5.html , https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_5.html

### 3a. Normally satisfactory system, isolated mistake (III.E.5.4)

13. "An isolated mistake by an assistant that happens in a normally satisfactory system is excusable." The party must plausibly show a normally effective system for monitoring time limits. Cited for this: J 2/86 and J 3/86 (OJ 1987, 362), J 23/92, T 428/98 (OJ 2001, 494), J 3/12, J 4/12. SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_4.html
14. T 1917/20: even a one-person office needs a routine control of critical mail-sorting acts. SOURCED (secondary, Case Law Book summary). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_4.html

### 3b. Cross-check in a large firm (III.E.5.4.4 a)

15. Where many dates are monitored, at least one effective cross-check is normally expected: J 9/86; also T 223/88, J 26/92, T 808/03, T 1149/11. SOURCED (secondary for each decision; the Case Law Book page itself is SOURCED). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_4_4_a.html
16. T 1962/08: monitoring must not rest on one person; the system needs a checking mechanism independent of the person who monitors time limits; this can be in one system or in a second, independent ("redundant") system. SOURCED (secondary, Case Law Book summary). Decision page https://www.epo.org/en/boards-of-appeal/decisions/t081962eu1 returned HTTP 404: NOT VERIFIED at decision level. https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_4_4_a.html
17. T 1465/07: an independent cross-check is not disproportionate for a large firm. Also cited (5.5.4 b) for: a new secretary needs supervision at shorter intervals. SOURCED (secondary). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_4_4_a.html
18. T 257/07 (decision text read): the system "relied exclusively on the conscientiousness of the person responsible" rather than an independent cross-check "that necessarily must include either another person or an automated system alerting another person". The computer system (IBIS) did not "actively monitor time limits and alert the person concerned". Outcome: re-establishment refused. SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/boards-of-appeal/decisions/pdf/t070257eu1.pdf (page: https://www.epo.org/en/boards-of-appeal/decisions/t070257eu1)
19. T 828/94 (catchword, as quoted in T 257/07): a monitoring system for EPO time limits "should contain an independent cross-check". SOURCED (secondary: quoted in T 257/07, decision not read). https://www.epo.org/boards-of-appeal/decisions/pdf/t070257eu1.pdf
20. T 686/97: a "redundant" or "failsafe system" is an essential part of a reminder system in corporate patent departments. T 261/07: a failsafe system needs regular comparison of the mail room database and the department database. SOURCED (secondary). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_4_4_a.html
21. J 9/16 (decision text read), point 10: the independent cross-check can be "a single database of time limits, which is checked independently by two different persons", or two independent databases "still required that they are handled by two different persons". SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/boards-of-appeal/decisions/pdf/j160009eu1.pdf
22. J 9/16, point 11: it is reasonable "to also expect an independent cross-check when data is entered" in a time-limit monitoring system, not only when recorded limits are monitored. Point 14: no "independent, i.e. parallel (or redundant), cross-check by a different person". SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/boards-of-appeal/decisions/pdf/j160009eu1.pdf
23. J 9/16, point 15: "it is not for the EPO to dictate how docketing and time limit monitoring must be organised"; applicants and representatives are free to organise their work. The Board names as one option ensuring "that a second person checked" the recording of new orders and time limits. SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/boards-of-appeal/decisions/pdf/j160009eu1.pdf
24. T 1823/16 (decision text read; electronic docketing system "Inprotech"), point 6: a cross-check must be at "the data entry phase, because if a mistake happens" there the case is not flagged. Point 8: in an electronic system, "if no data are entered the case becomes completely invisible"; what works for paper "might not be for the electronic world". Point 10: what all due care requires depends on the facts; the Board cannot prescribe one system; no referral to the Enlarged Board. Outcome: re-establishment rejected, appeal inadmissible. SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/boards-of-appeal/decisions/pdf/t161823eu1.pdf
25. Other "see also" decisions on the need for and form of an independent cross-check: T 1172/00, T 785/01, T 36/97, T 622/01, J 1/07, J 13/07. The Case Law Book page names them without stating holdings. SOURCED (secondary; holdings NOT VERIFIED). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_4_4_a.html
26. T 1726/08: a firm with 40 attorneys at four locations is not small. (The brief asked about "T 1726/17"; I did not find T 1726/17 in these sections. The decision the Case Law Book cites is T 1726/08.) SOURCED (secondary). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_4_4_a.html
27. T 836/09: for an isolated assistant mistake in outbound mail processing (low error risk), the duty to have an effective cross-check is dispensed with. SOURCED (secondary). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_4_4_a.html

### 3c. Small firms (III.E.5.4.4 b)

28. J 31/90, T 166/87: in a very small or relatively small office with reliable staff, a cross-check can be superfluous (T 166/87: especially for one-off payments such as an appeal fee). SOURCED (secondary). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_4_4_b.html
29. J 11/03: organisational needs vary with firm size and number of time limits; a cross-check may be superfluous in a small firm, not in a large company with its own patent department. SOURCED (secondary). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_4_4_b.html
30. T 428/98: stresses the exceptional nature of the small-office ruling; a six-person office needed a cross-checking mechanism. SOURCED (secondary). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_4_4_a.html
31. T 1355/09, T 3029/18: no control mechanism needed in a very small firm where the error was in paying the appeal fee, not in overlooking a time limit. SOURCED (secondary). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_4_4_b.html

### 3d. Computerised systems and software errors (III.E.5.4.7)

32. T 473/07 (decision text read), point 3.4: "program deficiencies are also a well-known part of that reality". "Relying exclusively on a time limit generated by a computer program" without even a plausibility check does not meet all due care. Point 3.5: a plausibility check "should have been carried out by the representative". Point 3.6: "all due care requires checking all steps" even when a computer system is used. Outcome: re-establishment refused. SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/boards-of-appeal/decisions/pdf/t070473eu1.pdf
33. T 902/05: where an applicant relies only on electronic means to record and monitor time limits, data backup "would generally be an elementary precaution". See also T 1663/12. SOURCED (secondary). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_4_7.html
34. T 1269/13 (4 February 2015): the computerised system in that case was considered satisfactory. The Case Law Book gives no reasoning. SOURCED (secondary; reasoning NOT VERIFIED). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_4_7.html
35. The brief named "T 1962/08?, T 2017/12?, J 4/07?" for computer systems. T 1962/08 is in the cross-check section (fact 16), not 5.4.7. J 4/07 is cited in Guidelines E-VIII 3.2; I did not find it in 5.4.7. T 2017/12 does not appear in the sections I read. NOT VERIFIED for any computer-specific holding of these three.

### 3e. Assistants: delegation, instruction, supervision (III.E.5.5.4)

36. Less rigorous standard for assistants doing routine tasks such as "typing dictated documents, posting letters and parcels and noting time limits" (J 5/80, OJ 1981, 343). The representative must have chosen "a suitable person properly instructed" and supervised reasonably. SOURCED (secondary for J 5/80). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_5_4_a.html
37. A representative cannot relieve himself of responsibility for tasks that fall to him personally (for example interpreting laws and treaties). SOURCED. https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_5_4_a.html
38. T 309/88: untrained staff may record time limits if properly instructed and a trained person is available. J 3/88: docket clerks need basic knowledge and close supervision in initial training; new assistants need regular supervision for several months. SOURCED (secondary). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_5_4_b.html
39. T 2450/16: preparing a notice of appeal is not routine; the EPO e-filing tool cannot replace instructions. T 2016/16: double-checking by a patent attorney (in a docketing system) does not discharge the duty to supervise. T 555/08: uncritical acceptance of an assistant's oral statements is not due care. SOURCED (secondary). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_5_4_b.html
40. T 439/06: once a file is presented to the representative, "responsibility passes over to him in all respects"; he must check time limits his records department calculated. T 1561/05: a final check on signing cannot be delegated so as to absolve the representative. R 18/13: the representative "could not simply assume that his assistants had reliably done all their work". J 25/96: routine monitoring may be delegated, but not particularly urgent cases with risk of irrevocable loss. T 592/11: representative liable for negligence in his own part of a double check. SOURCED (secondary). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_5_4_e.html
41. T 549/24 (new in 2026 revision): intentional deception by an assistant to bypass cross-check controls justified re-establishment; the monitoring system included an online tracking spreadsheet with reminders and weekly meetings. SOURCED (secondary). https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_5.html , https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_iii_e_5_5_4.html

## What the sources do not say

- No provision of the EPC, no Guidelines section and no Case Law Book section I read states that a software check may replace the second person in the independent cross-check. None states that it may not.
- Guidelines E-VIII 3.2 (2026) requires "an independent, effective cross-check mechanism" but does not say whether a person or a machine must perform it. It does not mention software at all.
- T 257/07 is the only text I found that names an automated system as part of a valid cross-check, and only as "an automated system alerting another person". The automated element there alerts a human; it does not replace the human decision. It is an obiter description in a refusal, not a holding that such a system was sufficient.
- J 9/16 (point 10) describes the cross-check as two different persons (one database checked by two persons, or two databases handled by two persons). It also says (point 15) that the EPO does not dictate how docketing must be organised. It does not address a machine-only check.
- T 473/07 says relying exclusively on computer-calculated time limits without a plausibility check fails due care, and that the representative should do the plausibility check. It does not say whether an automated second source (for example the EPO's own due dates) could serve as the plausibility check.
- T 1823/16 requires the cross-check at the data-entry stage in an electronic system. It does not say who or what must perform it, and says the Board cannot prescribe one system.
- No source I read deals with a rule set approved once by an attorney, with two independent automated readers, or with comparison against EPO-published due dates (for example MyEPO data).
- The re-establishment standard is assessed after a loss, on the facts of the system as it stood (T 1823/16 point 10). The sources set no ex-ante approval procedure for a docketing design.
- Decisions not read at decision level: T 1962/08 (page 404), T 828/94, T 686/97, T 261/07, T 1465/07, J 9/86, J 1/07, T 1269/13 and others marked SOURCED (secondary).

## Open legal question

Can a large firm meet the Art. 122 EPC "all due care" standard, and in particular the case-law requirement for an independent, effective cross-check (J 9/86, T 1962/08, J 9/16, T 1823/16), when the second, independent check of a time limit (at data entry and at monitoring) is done by software instead of a second person, if: (a) the event comes from a structured source or two independent readers agree, (b) the computation rule set was approved once by an attorney, and (c) the result agrees with a second independent date source such as EPO-published due dates; and with a human only when the checks disagree? The case law speaks of "another person or an automated system alerting another person" (T 257/07) and of checks by "two different persons" (J 9/16), and warns against exclusive reliance on computer-calculated dates (T 473/07). A European patent attorney must judge this. This file gives no conclusion.
